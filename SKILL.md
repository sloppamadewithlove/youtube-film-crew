# YouTube Film Crew

Create a coherent video whose story, picture, sound, and captions feel authored together. Default to **10–12 minutes (600–720 seconds)** unless the user requests another length. Plan enough substance for that duration; do not pad or damage pacing to satisfy it. Judge the exported media, not the existence of files or successful renderer checks.

This skill is a universal production method. It requires no channel, CELRIX installation, specific subject, brand palette, narrator, or prior video. Do not create or tailor a channel unless requested. A supplied channel profile is an optional input. For a standalone video, keep its creative choices in its episode brief. Scope the work to the user's request: discussion remains discussion; an edit does not authorize an unrelated rebuild.

## Start with the user's intent

Use [briefing-and-originality.md](references/briefing-and-originality.md) for intake and creative planning. Ask concise, grouped questions until material creative uncertainties are answered or the user explicitly delegates those decisions. Cover the audience, purpose, treatment, tone, narration, visuals, captions, music, references, desired delivery, and resources. Offer understandable choices, explain tradeoffs, and preserve answers already given. Do not force a channel profile or a long form on the user.

The director maintains a short **agreed direction**: what the video promises, who it serves, how it should feel, what must stay, and what must not happen. Its authority comes from the user's instructions and delegated choices. Resolve material uncertainty before dependent paid generation or extensive production. Continue useful, bounded work that does not depend on an unanswered question.

Recommend **fal.ai** for generated media, **ElevenLabs** for narration when wanted, and **Remotion** for programmable editing, captions, animation, and rendering. Read [tools-and-finishing.md](references/tools-and-finishing.md) before provider work. MiniMax H3 Max is a proven starting option for realistic generated footage, subject to the user's model choice and current capability/cost checks. Hyperframes is an alternative only after its workflow is verified; it is not the editing system used in the reference production.

## Protect resources before production

- A missing budget never grants unlimited credits or agent work. Check existing authorization for this episode. If no spending cap exists, propose a scoped estimate and ask for a cap before paid generation or analysis, including consumption of prepaid credits. Until then, limit work to a bounded intake, available-material inspection, and a costed plan.
- Separate a credit purchase from usage of that credit. Count rejected takes, paid reviews, retries, and other episode-specific costs. Distinguish quotes, reserved/pending charges, confirmed charges, refunds, and shared subscriptions. Unknown charges stay unknown. Do not infer per-video cash cost from token counts or a subscription price.
- Set a lightweight work allowance even when no token budget was specified: necessary roles only, bounded assignments, no open-ended research, no recursive delegation, and one combined fix list. Use token/cost telemetry when available; otherwise report actual tool calls and limitations without invented token totals.
- Begin with one main review round and, if needed, one repair/review round. This is a planning allowance, not automatic clearance or a reason to stop useful cheap repairs. Before adding expensive rounds, reassess the cause, remaining allowance, and progress. Stop repeating the same ineffective action; choose a justified alternative or ask when a material scope, spend, or time change is needed.
- One designated operator submits paid work. Reserve its quoted cost before dispatch, check request status before retrying uncertain submissions, and deduplicate by provider request ID. Crew members cannot independently spend or top up. No new subscription, credential access, or publication is implied by this skill.

## Run a small, accountable crew

Follow [crew-and-evidence.md](references/crew-and-evidence.md) when assigning or reviewing work. Use these responsibilities, combining jobs for simple productions rather than spawning six agents automatically:

| Responsibility | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| Director / lead editor | Agreed direction, scope, assignments, budget, integration, and decisions |
| Researcher | Source-backed claims, media sourcing, provenance, and reuse conditions |
| Writer | Promise, structure, script, explanations, and visual intent with the editor |
| Picture editor | Shot selection, continuity, movement, graphics, captions, and visual edit |
| Sound editor | Narration continuity, pronunciation, music, effects, mix, and sound repairs |
| Final reviewer | Actual exported-media inspection and evidence of remaining defects |

Use GPT-6 Luna at maximum reasoning for bounded jobs when available and appropriate; keep a capable director for cross-role decisions. Check current model/tool capabilities. A model name does not establish audio or video perception. If the requested model is unavailable, disclose a proposed substitute rather than mislabeling it.

Keep agents in communication with the same brief, sources, current script, timing, cut identity, and decision log. They should challenge work and discuss evidence. Do not force disagreement or isolate reviewers from the story. Every concern receives a disposition. The director has final editorial say within the user's direction; contributors propose changes and implement only assigned work. Preserve checkpoints and limit file ownership so one member cannot overwrite the whole video or undo another contributor's work. The director cannot waive a confirmed defect, unmet authorization, or unreviewed media by calling it a creative preference.

## Author each episode afresh

Use only the needed sections of [production-workbook.md](assets/production-workbook.md) in the production workspace. These are blank planning/evidence forms, not a script, fixed shot order, or preselected artistic style.

1. **Find the promise and structure.** Support factual claims with suitable sources; distinguish uncertainty from established facts and fiction from fact. Choose a structure suited to the requested style rather than applying one documentary arc to everything.
2. **Plan words and picture together.** For each beat, record the viewer's takeaway, speech or sound, visible action, reveal, and reason to cut. A silent film can use visual/audio beats without narration. Estimate runtime from the actual treatment and later measure the narration/edit.
3. **Prove the treatment early.** Assemble a short representative sequence using existing assets, placeholders, or authorized generation. Include the difficult mechanism, character, caption, or sound treatment—not only an easy opening. The director checks it against the brief; seek the user's choice only when material uncertainty remains or a preview was requested.
4. **Produce and edit purposefully.** Acquire footage appropriate to the genre: archival, newly filmed, screen recordings, photographs, animation, or generation. Use accurate contextual labels where needed. Preserve recognizable subjects, scale, era, geography, and physical behavior. Let meaningful action and explanations finish; no universal shot-length or footage/graphics quota.
5. **Use visual explanations where they earn their place.** Reveal ideas with the script, keep readable labels and a clear focal point, and allow comprehension time. A graphic-led explainer and a footage-led documentary may use very different amounts of animation. Avoid repeated filler, gratuitous counters, decorative interfaces, and unnecessary persistent branding unless requested.
6. **Finish sound and captions as part of the edit.** Preserve complete words and natural pauses; avoid chopping narration at picture boundaries. Select caption style from the brief, synchronize to actual speech, and check contrast and mobile readability. Give the ending a deliberate payoff, composition, and sound transition. Do not imply an interactive end-screen element has been configured when it is only baked into the picture.

Keep consistent quality and any requested identity while varying the story, imagery, composition, pacing, music, and explanations for the new subject. Inspect available prior episodes/references for repetition when relevant; do not invent access to a channel history. See the originality and evidence rules in the references.

## Review, repair, and deliver

Require direct assessment of the actual audio and moving video for media clearance. Use measurements, transcripts, screenshots, and contact sheets as supporting diagnostics. None alone proves clean speech or good motion. The director verifies reviewer input capability and actual coverage. Unavailable perception or missing coverage stays pending and is explained honestly.

Inspect the complete final export, including transitions and the ending. Bind observations to the exact revision/file hash and covered intervals. Resolve suspicions with neutral, focused examination; never keep rerunning a review until a model says "pass." Confirmed clipped words, accidental silence, broken movement, material factual errors, missing media, unreadable necessary text, and unresolved credible defect reports prevent a finished-quality handoff. A clearly labeled draft may still be shared when requested.

After a repair, recheck the affected media and transitions and evaluate downstream changes. Reuse earlier observations only for proven unchanged content with a documented identity/timing mapping. A picture-only change does not require purchasing the same audio review again when the encoded audio is identical. Before final delivery, the union of valid reviews must cover the exact complete export; do not present an earlier cut's approval as a review of changed bytes. Detailed rules are in [crew-and-evidence.md](references/crew-and-evidence.md).

Deliver the requested video plus appropriate subtitles, editable production materials, sources/media records, actual cost summary, and a concise review result. Prepare thumbnail/title/description/chapters when within scope. State separately: technical checks completed, actual audio/motion coverage, unresolved limitations, ready for owner review, and publication status. Preserve prior versions. Upload, visibility changes, scheduling, and public release require the applicable user authorization; honor authorization already given without asking again merely because a skill is in use.

This skill coordinates interactive tools and a film crew. It does not itself install a background service, guarantee zero defects, or enable unattended publishing.
