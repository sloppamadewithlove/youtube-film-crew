# Crew coordination, disagreement, and media evidence

Read this when delegating, integrating changes, or deciding whether a video is ready for review.

## Assign narrowly; share the story

Create only the roles needed for this production. A director, combined researcher/writer, picture editor, music director/music editor, sound editor, and reviewer may be appropriate; a small edit may require fewer. Assign a dedicated music role when music substantially shapes a full production, and combine it with sound editing for simpler work. Do not automatically launch all roles or permit unbounded nested agents.

The music director/music editor owns musical progression, passage selection, and timing instructions for development, fades, stops, and returns. The picture editor coordinates the visual timing, and the sound editor owns narration, effects, music integration, and the final combined mix. Keep the music role involved from planning through actual soundtrack review, with timestamped changes routed through the director; the final reviewer still assesses the complete experience. The designated operator remains responsible for all paid submissions.

Every assignment identifies the current brief and script, deliverable, owned files/responsibility, excluded actions, model/capabilities, work allowance, and expected evidence. Tell editing workers they are not alone, must not revert others' work, and must communicate interface/timing changes. One integrator owns the final timeline/master. Reviewers have read-only access to protected production files unless assigned a specific repair.

Provide shared context appropriate to the role: agreed direction, sources and rights, beat map, current script, narration/timing, selected media, current-cut identity, and decision log. All media reviewers must know the storyline and what the edit is trying to achieve. Keep their judgment their own. A colleague's "pass" or "fail" is not a substitute for checking the artifact.

Notify affected roles when a change alters facts, a sentence, narration timing, asset identity, a graphic, or a transition. Route requested changes through the director. When a child finishes, close/interrupt it according to the host's agent lifecycle tools; do not leave completed workers consuming attention or awaiting duplicate work.

## Constructive disagreement protocol

Ask each role to identify the most consequential weakness, if any, and explain its judgment. Do not reward the number of objections or require an invented defect. Honest agreement after a check is valid.

Every concern records:

- Stable issue ID, author/role, exact artifact revision/hash, and timestamp/range or script passage.
- Observed problem, evidence/source, and why it matters to this video's purpose.
- Category: confirmed defect, suspected defect needing inspection, creative suggestion, or scope/authorization issue.
- Severity, proposed smallest effective fix, affected roles, and any expected cost/timing impact.
- Director's disposition, owner, and verification result after any change.

Examples:

- "At 02:10.2, the exported word 'pressure' loses its final syllable; source take is complete. Restore the tail and inspect the transition" is an actionable sound concern.
- "This section feels slow" needs a cited interval and a reason tied to viewer understanding or the agreed treatment before it warrants an edit.
- "Replace the calm tutorial with a thriller" changes the agreed direction and cannot be imposed by a reviewer.

The author/editor responds with evidence or a proposed repair. The director decides **accept**, **accept a smaller change**, **reject with reason**, or **unresolved pending evidence**. Preserve disagreement and reasoning; do not rewrite the log to erase objections. The reviewer may point out new evidence or an unaddressed consequence, but cannot repeatedly relitigate the same taste question.

The director may commission one narrowly scoped additional check when evidence conflicts. Escalate to the user only for material changes to their intent, permissions/resources, genuinely unresolved preference, or a blocker requiring their action. The director decides creative tradeoffs; voting does not clear technical, factual, or authorization defects. A credible reported defect can pause the affected delivery decision while examined, but does not authorize its reporter to rewrite the entire video.

## Protect the work from a damaging suggestion

Preserve the agreed direction, original media, editable sources, and a recoverable version before substantial changes. Test disputed creative changes in a copy or short excerpt where practical. Compare them against the brief and actual media, including downstream sound, timing, and story effects. Only the director/integrator adopts the change. Prefer the smallest repair that solves the supported problem.

No member can silently replace the script, narrator, style, whole timeline, or another member's work. Final say does not allow the director to certify an unheard soundtrack, ignore a verified clipped word, waive a needed permission, or contradict an explicit user constraint. Their responsibility is to choose and verify a valid resolution.

## Qualify the reviewer before spending on reviews

Check that the chosen tool accepts the actual audio/video input and provides the necessary perceptual capability. A text-only model can review a script or interpret measurements but cannot certify the soundtrack or motion. Sampling still frames does not establish moving-video coverage.

Use neutral review requests that state the intended experience, exact interval, and what to inspect. Do not tell the reviewer to find no problems or to confirm a desired verdict. Do not treat a successful upload or a generic model summary as proof the entire input was inspected.

Record the input file hash, duration, source cut, review tool/model, request ID when available, submitted interval, demonstrated coverage, findings, and limitations. Verify returned coverage: an account describing only the opening of a long clip is partial, even if the whole file was uploaded. Fill gaps with continuous, overlapping excerpts. Mark artificial excerpt boundaries so they are not mistaken for cuts in the film. Locate alleged defects by exact words or visible actions as well as estimated times.

Use a known problematic short sample to calibrate a new review path when justified; do not repeat calibration for a proven unchanged workflow. Keep failed requests and initial concerns. Their absence from the final cut does not erase their cost or the need to explain resolution.

## What to inspect

**Research/story:** promise and payoff; supported claims; factual/fictional treatment; correct source context; useful progression; understandable technical language; narration-picture agreement.

**Picture in motion:** purposeful coverage and cuts; readable explanations; continuity of objects, people, location, era, proportions, and action; generated distortions; repeated filler; accidental freezes/blank frames; caption collisions; coherent ending. A deliberate static composition or silence is assessed against intent, not automatically rejected.

**Sound directly:** complete words and natural breathing/pauses; pronunciation and voice consistency; every edit/join; clicks, crackles, stutters, duplicated speech, gaps, and abrupt music changes; voice intelligibility; intended sound perspective and ending. Inspect source speech, assembly, and mix when diagnosing a defect. Do not compress speech or discard syllables to force a scene duration.

**Captions:** faithful spoken text, proper names, timing, readable phrase grouping, intentional emphasis, safe placement, contrast, and legibility at realistic phone size. Word-by-word animation and a particular color are creative choices, not universal rules.

**Technical diagnostics:** decode integrity, dimensions/aspect/frame rate, expected duration, tracks and sync, peak/clipping/loudness measurements, missing assets, and unexplained blank/silent intervals. Diagnostics support perception and never replace it. Retain the measured result and applied preset rather than claiming that a successful encode means editorial quality passed.

## Changes, coverage, and efficient rechecks

Review the complete master sound and exported soundtrack with direct audio capability, and the complete export in motion with suitable visual capability. A capable combined inspection may support both only when its evidence actually covers both modalities. Keep a coverage table per artifact and modality, including gaps and intervals that could not be assessed.

After a change, identify what changed and its dependencies. Recheck changed intervals with transition handles and any affected downstream timing, captions, facts, or story connections. Larger restructuring or uncertain mapping requires broader review.

An unchanged audio payload can retain its earlier direct review after a picture-only replacement. For unchanged picture intervals, document identical media and timing or another appropriate verified identity chain; reuse only the observations that evidence supports. If re-encoding changes bytes, investigate with suitable decoded comparisons and explicit mapping rather than assuming equality. Never silently carry an approval to altered content.

Final coverage must span the exact delivered export from opening through ending, with no unreviewed changes or unresolved credible defect reports. An uninterrupted full watch remains the normal final editorial check when feasible; inherited technical/audio evidence can prevent unnecessary duplicate paid analyses. If a tool cannot provide complete perception, state that limit and keep finished-quality clearance pending. Do not promise that model review guarantees zero errors.

## Finish without endless debate

Consolidate findings into one prioritized repair list: blocking defects, improvements needed to meet the brief, then optional polish. Fix the first two; optional preferences do not reopen completed work indefinitely. Apply the episode's work/spend limits and review allowance. Repeated disagreements need new evidence, not additional identical reviewers.

Report statuses separately: technical checks, direct audio coverage, direct motion coverage, editorial issues resolved, owner review ready, owner accepted, and upload/publication state. A draft can be shared honestly as a draft. Public release never follows automatically from a reviewer's pass.
